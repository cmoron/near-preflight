near-preflight

The journal of one demonstration guard, …, a contract on NEAR testnet. Each line is a purchase (or a sale) an AI agent asked for, most often the demo's scripted agent, and what the guard answered before NEAR would sign anything; a line names the model decision behind it when one is attached.

The demo's tokens live on a local fork of Base (anvil), not on Base: the traps and the buys run there, and the guard's signed transactions are broadcast only to that fork. Checks from check-mainnet.sh simulate USDC on Base mainnet, read-only, and nothing is ever sent to Base. The verdicts, checks and blocklist below are real, read from NEAR testnet.

How one check goes

  1. The agent asks. It names a token and an amount, and pays for the check.
  2. The sentinel simulates. An off-chain service runs the buy NEAR would sign, then two sales of what it bought, and judges the token.
  3. The verdict is stored on NEAR. With the sha256 of its evidence and the hash of the one transaction the guard may sign.
  4. NEAR signs. On allow, or a warn the owner approved, the guard builds the swap itself and NEAR Chain Signatures signs it.

The evidence replays: the Base-mainnet check shipped in the repository on any machine, a fork check against the team's fork and proof files (replay and attest).
